About

Youngjun Lee, Ph.D., is a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Division of Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia and an affiliated postdoctoral researcher in the Annenberg Extended Reality Lab at the University of Pennsylvania. His research examines how physical activity, sport, and sleep relate to depression, anxiety, and suicidal behavior in children and youth, with a focus on autism and developmental disabilities. He works with national surveys, longitudinal cohorts, and pediatric health records, and designs immersive virtual reality exercise programs for autistic young adults. He earned his Ph.D. in Kinesiology from Michigan State University, where his dissertation tested a virtual reality boxing program for autistic young adults, and his master's and bachelor's degrees in physical education from Korea University.

Research

Depression is one of the most common co-occurring conditions for autistic people, and it often begins in adolescence. Physical activity and sport are among the most accessible ways to protect mental health, but we still know little about when, and for whom, they protect autistic young people. Most exercise programs are also not built around autistic people's sensory, social, and motivational needs.

My research takes on both problems. With national surveys, birth cohorts, and pediatric health records, I follow physical activity, sport, sleep, and mental health from early childhood to early adulthood. With immersive virtual reality, I design exercise programs that autistic young adults want to keep doing. I built and tested a VR boxing program during my Ph.D. at Michigan State University, and I continue both lines of work at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia and the Annenberg Extended Reality Lab at Penn.

My ultimate goal

To make physical activity an accessible, evidence-based path to better mental health for every autistic person.

Google Scholar profile

Physical activity and mental health across development

Population, cohort, and clinical epidemiology

I study how physical activity, sport participation, and sleep relate to depression, anxiety, and suicidal behavior from early childhood into early adulthood, and how these links differ for autistic and non-autistic youth and for people with developmental disabilities. Much of this work follows the same young people over years and asks what makes activity protective, looking at bullying, adversity, screen time, and caregiver mental health.

National surveys and cohorts

NSCH, NHANES, HBSC, KYRBS, the UK Millennium Cohort Study, and Korean national panels, across U.S., UK, Korean, and cross-national samples.

Pediatric health records

Wellness-visit records at CHOP, linking physical activity screening in primary care to depression in adolescents.

Measurement and methods

Device-measured activity and sleep, multiverse and latent class analyses, and simulation studies of missing data.

Virtual reality exercise

Digital behavioral health interventions

I design immersive virtual reality exergames that lower the sensory and social demands of gyms and team sports, using virtual coaches to create a sense of social presence. My two pilot randomized trials of VR boxing tested its effects on depressive symptoms and heart rate variability, and my current work focuses on who stays engaged, why, and how the body responds.

Virtual coaching

Coach-induced social presence as a way to sustain engagement and support mood.

Engagement and retention

Mixed-methods process evaluation of who stays in VR exercise, who leaves, and why.

Physiology and tolerability

Heart rate variability, simulator sickness, and interest among autistic and non-autistic users.

Data and methods

Datasets
National Survey of Children's Health (NSCH), NHANES, Health Behaviour in School-aged Children (HBSC), Korea Youth Risk Behavior Survey (KYRBS), KNHANES, UK Millennium Cohort Study, Korean national panel studies, NIH All of Us Research Program, pediatric electronic health records
Study designs
Longitudinal cohort and panel analyses, multiverse and latent class analyses, simulation studies, pilot randomized controlled trials, mixed-methods process evaluations, systematic and scoping reviews following PRISMA
Measures
Physical activity, sport participation, and sleep; depression, anxiety, and suicidal behavior; device-measured activity (Fitbit, GENEActiv, ActiGraph); heart rate variability; blood biomarkers
Open science
Preregistered secondary data analyses and reviews on OSF
Tools
R, SPSS, Python, Meta Quest
