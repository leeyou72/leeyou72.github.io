Postdoctoral Research Fellow

Department of Pediatrics · Division of Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition
Children's Hospital of Philadelphia
Affiliated Researcher · Annenberg Extended Reality Lab, University of Pennsylvania

I am a postdoctoral researcher working to reduce depression and promote physical activity in autistic individuals through technology-driven approaches. My research combines two complementary streams: intervention studies using extended reality (XR) and wearable devices to design accessible exercise programs, and population-level epidemiological analyses of large-scale datasets (NHANES, NSCH, NIH All of Us) to uncover behavioral and physiological markers of depression. Across both lines of work, I aim to bridge the gap between technology, physical activity science, and mental health to improve the lives of individuals on the autism spectrum.

Research Interests

Autism & Depression

Understanding the mechanisms linking physical inactivity to depression in autistic individuals, and developing evidence-based strategies to improve mental health outcomes across the lifespan.

XR & Wearable Interventions

Designing extended reality (XR) exergames and leveraging wearable sensor technology to create accessible, engaging physical activity interventions tailored for autistic populations.

Physical Activity Epidemiology

Conducting population-level analyses using large-scale datasets (NHANES, NSCH, NIH All of Us) to identify activity and sleep patterns associated with depressive outcomes.

Wearable-Derived Health Markers

Analyzing accelerometer and Fitbit data to characterize behavioral rhythms and physiological indicators that may predict or reflect depression in adults with and without autism.
